Car Electrical Problems and ECU Diagnostics: What to Check First

Electrical faults can cause hard starting, warning lights, intermittent stalling, sensor codes, communication errors or a vehicle that will not crank. The same symptom can have several causes. Use the checks below to gather useful evidence before replacing parts.

Battery, charging and starting problems

If the engine cranks slowly, clicks, or the dashboard resets, begin with battery condition, terminal security, corrosion, main grounds and the starter circuit. If the engine starts but the battery warning remains on, the charging system and wiring need testing. A single voltage reading is not always enough: battery load, charging behaviour and voltage drop under load may matter.

Stop and seek professional help if cables heat up, smoke appears, the battery is swollen or leaking, or there is a burning smell. Do not short battery terminals or bypass high-current protection.

Check-engine light, sensor faults and DTC codes

Record the exact diagnostic trouble code (DTC), including any suffix, and note whether it is current, pending or stored. Save freeze-frame data and relevant live data when the scan tool supports them. A sensor-related code does not automatically prove the sensor has failed: check its power supply, ground, connector, wiring and the conditions described by the vehicle-specific service procedure.

Start with our ECU fault-code diagnosis guide and car warning-light and symptom guide.

ECU not communicating with the scan tool

Confirm the scan tool supports the vehicle and protocol. Check the diagnostic connector for visible damage, the relevant fuses and the vehicle battery voltage. Communication problems may involve the diagnostic port, wiring, a network fault, a module that is not powered, or scan-tool compatibility. Network testing should follow the correct wiring diagram and service procedure; do not randomly bridge diagnostic connector pins.

Intermittent faults after rain, washing or repairs

Record when the fault appears and whether it follows rain, engine heat, vibration, battery replacement or recent repairs. Inspect accessible connectors and harnesses for visible damage or water intrusion only when safe. Avoid probing sealed connectors or safety-system wiring without the proper equipment.

Diesel, DPF, EGR and emissions warnings

Emissions-system warnings can be caused by operating conditions, pressure or temperature sensors, wiring, exhaust restrictions or other system faults. Diagnose the underlying cause using vehicle-specific data and legal repair procedures. Do not disable emissions controls as a substitute for diagnosis.

Airbag and SRS warnings

Airbags and pretensioners are safety-critical. Do not test SRS circuits with an ordinary test lamp, probe airbag connectors, or clear crash data as a substitute for inspecting deployed or damaged components. Follow the manufacturer's isolation and repair procedures or use a qualified technician. See our airbag module information.

Information to collect before asking a workshop

  • Vehicle make, model, year, engine and fuel type.
  • Exact symptom and when it happens (cold, hot, under load, after rain or intermittently).
  • Full DTC code and status, plus freeze-frame or live-data readings if available.
  • Recent repairs, battery work, jump-starts or accessory installations.
  • Any measurements already taken and the tool used.

Do not post registration numbers, VINs, customer names, phone numbers or other personal details in public diagnostic discussions.

Frequently asked questions

Should I replace a part because a fault code names it?

No. A DTC identifies a detected condition or circuit. Confirm the fault with the correct wiring diagram, measurements and diagnostic procedure before replacing parts.

Can a weak battery cause several warning lights?

Low or unstable voltage can contribute to multiple warnings or communication symptoms, but the cause should be verified with battery and charging-system tests rather than assumed.

What if the vehicle loses power or shows a critical warning?

If braking or steering assistance is affected, the engine overheats, oil pressure is low, smoke appears, or the vehicle behaves dangerously, stop in a safe place and arrange qualified assistance.
